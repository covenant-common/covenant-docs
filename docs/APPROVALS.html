Approve your agent

This is planned. The public tools cannot take a passkey approval, and no hosted method can pay. The story below is how approval is meant to work once the registry says it is available.

Covenant never holds your money. In test mode, the test rail holds it; for real payments, the payment provider or an escrow contract will.

The short version

  1. Your agent asks for permission.
  2. You see the all-in summary on a Covenant page, not in the chat. That page is the Trusted Surface.
  3. You approve with your passkey.
  4. Your agent can act only inside the limits you signed: who the seller is, the most you will pay, and when the permission ends.

The seller is who you are buying from. Covenant provides the platform. A chat message is not an approval.

Open and closed

An open mandate is a limit you set before the exact basket exists. Example: this seller, at most £40, before Friday. A closed mandate is a yes to one exact checkout or one exact amount. AP2 v0.2 uses a Checkout Mandate and a Payment Mandate. Each one can be open or closed. IntentMandate and CartMandate are previous names.

If you reject the summary, nothing is reserved and the agent cannot use that permission. You can also revoke a permission you already gave. Revocation stops a later use. It does not unwind a payment that has already left the payment provider or the escrow contract.

For agents: do not treat chat as a yes

There is no public tool that creates, signs or revokes a mandate. Do not invent a passkey result. When this is built, the signed mandate comes from the Trusted Surface, and a later call must stay inside it. A rejection returns no usable mandate.
