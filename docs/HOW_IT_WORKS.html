Make the job clear

Say what you need, when you need it and how you will review the result. For the report, that means three suppliers, checkable sources and a comparison table by Friday.

Agree one version of the deal

  1. Make an offer. Name the parties, work, price, deadline and review process.
  2. Discuss changes. Record a new version when the price or scope changes.
  3. Collect the required approvals. Each approval names the same version. The record shows whose approval is still missing.
  4. Follow the work. Add delivery, supporting material and feedback beside the accepted terms.
  5. Finish what remains. Record satisfactory review, corrections or further work still owed.

A small exchange can stop at one offer and one acceptance. Larger jobs can require several parties without changing this basic pattern.

Choose what your agent may do

Give the purchasing agent a role, budget and conditions. In a connected workflow, an action beyond those limits is refused or sent for further approval.

A supplier’s message cannot grant your agent more authority. If a proposal exceeds its budget, the authorised person reviews that exact proposal before it can proceed.

Compare proposals before making a commitment

Invite suppliers to bid and compare price, scope and delivery time. A bid or shortlist records a possibility. The agreement proceeds only when the parties required by its rules approve the chosen terms.

For continuing work, reuse the relationship and add a fresh order. For a team, assign buying, delivery and review roles. Connect the tools, files and payment services the job needs during pilot setup.

Keep delivery and review separate

The report arrives, but its comparison table is missing. Record the delivery and identify the unfinished work against the agreed terms. A receipt shows that something arrived; the reviewer still needs to decide whether it satisfies the job.

The parties can request a correction or follow their agreed dispute process. Keep the relevant evidence and decisions together. Signatures can identify the recorded approvals; checking the quality of the work and observing an outside payment require the appropriate people or connected services.

Continue from the record

Save the agreement and reopen it after an interruption. If a connected payment service loses its reply, use its recovery checks to find the original result before retrying. A receipt should not create a second charge or erase unfinished work.

Choose who may see supporting material. The full implementation has tools for private evidence and offline work; those connections need their own setup. The demos use sample information and require no token or blockchain.

Try the rules in four sample workflows

The advanced demo offers four separate examples. Choose one and take each role to see why the next step is allowed, refused or waiting for someone else.

  1. Delegate a purchase. Give an agent buying limits, try a proposal outside its authority and see why new permission is needed.
  2. Coordinate several parties. Agree a project that needs several approvals. See who has approved the same terms and who is still needed. When terms change, choose their effect and collect fresh approvals.
  3. Compare competing quotes. Inspect offers, choose a supplier and see why selection and acceptance are separate steps.
  4. Review a disputed delivery. Use the agreed review role, consider the supporting records and record the next duties. Continue with a correction, customer review and separate payment reports.

Each example keeps its own signed history. The standalone demo uses real signatures from sample keys and enforces the rules shown in the page. You simulate all participants. It makes no live payments and does not establish compatibility with every part of the full implementation.
