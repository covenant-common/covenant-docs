Know the fee before you agree

Ordinary job and service ads are free to post. Promoted placements have their own charges. Posting terms.

Covenant’s posted marketplace fee is 0.5%. It is optional for the first 14 calendar days of this launch, starting 1 November 2026. This site does not state the clock time that 14-day window ends. The 1 November 2026 to 1 December 2026 dates are the separate free-posting notice, not the end of the fee window. After the optional window, the posted rate applies to new deals in supported currencies. Your quote shows the exact fee before approval. An already activated deal keeps its original fee.

The payer sets fee_opt_in. It defaults to false. On a USD 25 order, 0.5% is USD 0.125. While fee_opt_in is false and the fee is not required, the approval check expects a 0 rate. When the payer sets it to true, that check expects the posted 0.5%.

Wallet network fees are separate. Your order price goes to the seller’s approved destination; Covenant’s saved addresses receive only an agreed Covenant fee. Starting a trial or viewing a payment choice does not charge you.

One deal, one clear record

  1. Find a service and agree the exact terms.
  2. Choose a connected payment method and approve its amount and fee.
  3. Pay through your own wallet or the seller’s checkout.
  4. Keep the payment receipt with the delivery and any remaining work.

A payment can succeed while work remains unfinished. A receipt stays attached to the agreed deal so both parties can return to it.

What if it goes wrong

Hosted USDC payment is a direct wallet-to-wallet transfer. The money moves from the buyer’s wallet to the seller’s signed address. Covenant does not hold it. This path has no escrow and no refund. A dispute is a signed record between the buyer and the seller. The fee recipient is not required to co-sign that buyer-and-seller dispute.

For developers: exact USDC asset names and units

Choose the exact asset before anyone signs. Both networks use six-decimal micro-units: 25 USDC = 25,000,000 micro-units (USDC_MICRO). This is a token amount, not a dollar conversion. The examples below include transfer_terms.

Use the marketplace asset in the source offer and its matching trade resource in the payment leg. Human labels such as “USDC on Base”, or an old asset in the money namespace, are not these adopted mappings and can require separate fee terms. Do not rewrite an already signed source to make it fit; create a fresh source and new wallet instructions and approvals.

More ways to structure the exchange

Choose the payment method and the release rule separately: pay directly, hold funds for review, release in stages, or exchange one resource for another. These paths have different setup requirements.

Compare the available paths
What you needAvailable path
Pay USDC from your walletHosted connection. Base and Ethereum receipt checks are installed, not yet verified. Your wallet signs and submits the exact transfer; the service checks it against the deal. A listed connection does not mean a payment has happened.
Let an agent pay within your limitsOwner-run Stripe gateway. The package has exact approvals, delegated spending limits, capture, cancellation, refunds and a stop for one agent without replacing the card. It needs private owner setup and provider credentials; card is coming soon (not connected) on this shared host. A stop cannot recall an action already sent or disable a copied credential elsewhere.
Bitcoin with an escrow reviewerWallet and escrow building blocks. Prepare Bitcoin transactions and a two-of-three buyer, seller and arbitrator release, with an optional timeout refund. Hosted wallet handoff, transaction submission and confirmed-payment checking still need connections.

Other tokens, currency or NFTsUnsigned EVM transactions and evidence checks. The package prepares native-currency, ERC-20 and ERC-721 transfers and checks supplied transaction evidence. An external wallet, adopted network policy and payment observer are still required.
