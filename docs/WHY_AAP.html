Know what an agent agreed to, who authorised it and what remains owed.

Agentic Agreements Protocol (AAP), developed as Covenant, is an agreement and evidence layer for people and their agents. It is useful when obligations must survive a chat session, a model change, a supplier change or an interrupted request. Its practical contribution is the connection between exact terms, authority, delivery evidence, adopted payment observations and remaining duties.

Keep your tools; make the agreement inspectable.

Your chosen agent harness can coordinate conversation; MCP/A2A or another adopted interface can carry calls; a credential/execution service can control a particular effect. AAP supplies exact agreement records and the evidence/procedure links around that work. Each integration retains its own version, installation, trust and execution requirements.

This is a way to compose with existing infrastructure. It does not establish that every alternative lacks agreements, that any protocol depends on AAP, or that AAP is already superior in adoption, cost or security. An ordinary low-risk conversation may need less machinery; add the checks your actual consequence requires.

Five concrete design choices

  1. Exact assent across revisions. Selected profiles bind signatures to the adopted terms, context and role. A changed price or scope receives its own fingerprint and requires the approvals specified by the adopted profile. Old signatures cannot silently approve changed terms.
  2. The principal and the actual actor remain visible. An owner can delegate a scoped role without handing over its key. Representation carries the grant chain, role, context and time limits; current-authority assessment remains conditional on a separately adopted state source and clock.
  3. Records can leave their original application. Parties can carry terms, signatures, references and evidence to a declared verifier. Separate Python/Node readers exercise specific covered profiles; an unsupported profile must remain unsupported rather than be described as verified.
  4. Recovery preserves the original request. Adopted workflows retain exact identities and uncertainty rather than creating a new request after every lost reply. Tested local cases exercise restart, idempotent retry, partition/healing, disputes, partial awards and appeals under their declared assumptions.
  5. Complexity is optional and versioned. Start with one offer and acceptance. Add linked calloffs, milestones, guarantors, private disclosures, adopted proof mechanisms or ledger/courier carriers where needed. A new carrier or interface does not silently rewrite an old agreement's meaning.

These are implemented design choices with finite local evidence. They are not a guarantee of legal effect, real asset title, current global state, physical delivery or external payment.

Designed to keep useful meaning when technology changes

Small versioned records, explicit dependencies, independently checkable fingerprints and declared interpretation rules make migration inspectable. Owners can adopt a new connector or verification mechanism without pretending it was part of an earlier signature. The code still carries documented layering/dependency debt; this is a continuing engineering programme, not proof of indefinite survival.

Reusable procedures, saved exact templates and contextual discovery can help later participants start from useful work. Reuse creates a fresh proposal and requires explicit adoption. Popularity or reputation never supplies signing or execution authority. Real retention, network effects, savings and willingness to pay still require external measurement.
