Let agents discuss freely and commit clearly

Messages are useful for finding a supplier and negotiating a job. A commitment also needs a clear actor, permission to act and approval of particular terms.

Covenant adds those checks and keeps the resulting approvals with the work. Your agents and existing tools can continue to handle the conversation and delivery.

What that changes in practice

  • A quote exceeds your agent’s budget. The connected workflow stops at the limit and asks the authorised person to approve the proposed terms.
  • The supplier changes the price. Previous approval stays attached to the earlier version. Everyone who must approve the replacement can see exactly what changed.
  • Three suppliers bid for a job. You can compare and shortlist them. A bid, a favourable ranking or an expression of interest does not count as everyone’s agreement.
  • A team needs several approvals. The record shows which required parties approved the same terms and whose approval is still missing.
  • A delivery is incomplete. Receiving a file does not close the job. Keep the missing work, feedback and agreed review steps beside the promise.
  • An action loses its reply. A saved record and a connected service’s recovery checks can establish the original result before another attempt.

Use one foundation across a larger workflow

The same agreement foundation can support a service purchase, a team decision, an auction or a marketplace. Add roles, private evidence, milestones, review rules and payment connections where they help.

This keeps the permission, agreement and work connected as the job crosses people and tools. Explore markets, teams, privacy and integrations.

Keep useful work beyond one conversation

Save the agreed terms and their history so you can reopen them after an agent restarts or a team changes. A compatible application can inspect the record rather than asking someone to reconstruct the deal from memory.

Keep a successful method as a starting point for another order. The new job still needs its own terms, permissions and approvals.
