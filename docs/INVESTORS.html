The agreement gets scattered across tools

A brief lives in a conversation, approval arrives by email and delivery appears elsewhere. A changed price, missing reply or agent restart can leave people unsure what was approved and what remains to be done.

Keep the deal with the work

Covenant records who may act, which terms each party approved and what happened next. A small job needs one offer and acceptance. A longer relationship can add spending limits, recurring orders, milestones, delivery evidence and review rules. Basic agreements do not require a blockchain or project token.

An agent service could commission a report within a customer’s budget, request approval for a price change, record a partial delivery and check a lost reply before retrying. The next step is to test that workflow in a customer’s systems. See the capabilities.

Start with developers running agent services

Our first commercial focus is developers and platforms that need agreement software and help connecting it to their services. Research and analysis provide a concrete first workflow: a brief, price, delivery and reviewer.

The developer or platform would license a deployment for its service. The annual fee is not a charge on each report its customers buy.

Sell software, support and integration

The proposed beta licence is USD 7,200 a year for one deployment, including five executors and direct support. Executors carry out approved actions. Integration and operation need an agreed scope. The plan is not yet open for purchase. See pricing and terms.

Commercial use of covered core code requires a paid written licence, including business evaluation and internal use. Earlier grants and third-party licences remain in force. The public documentation and standalone demos have separate MIT licences.

Prove the value in repeated work

A connection becomes more useful if it supports several services. An agreement becomes more useful if it supports repeat orders and can move between compatible tools. Customers need a measurable gain: less time checking approvals, clearer handoffs or better recovery after an interruption.

The commercial test is whether customers will pay enough to cover deployment and support, then keep using it.

The next milestones

  • Complete a customer pilot. Agree the job, owner, setup, cost and success measures. Track completion, recovery, time spent clarifying work and repeat use.
  • Establish willingness to pay. Test who buys, the price they accept and the cost to deploy and support them.
  • Qualify the software for live use. Review security independently and test the integrations, failure handling and operating load the customer needs.
