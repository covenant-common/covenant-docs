The thesis

More autonomous work creates more consequential commitments. A buyer still needs to know what was authorised, a supplier needs to know what counts as acceptance, and both need a usable record when something goes wrong. Our thesis is that portable agreements and evidence can become useful shared infrastructure across otherwise separate agent platforms.

The first opportunities

  • Paid knowledge and services: research, freelance delivery and procurement with explicit acceptance criteria.
  • Enterprise agent access: bounded delegation, verification and auditable outside-agent interactions.
  • Content and data licensing: agreed scope, purpose, payment evidence and receipts for particular uses.

These are target workflows for pilots, not claims of deployed customer contracts or a validated market size.

How the ecosystem could compound

Reusable agreement patterns, integrations and portable evidence could lower the cost of working with unfamiliar counterparties. More useful implementations could make the same records easier to exchange across services. That is an adoption hypothesis to test through repeated use; it is not yet a measured network effect.

What progress should demonstrate

  1. External pilot participants can complete useful work and recover from failure with less friction.
  2. Design partners use the system repeatedly and can identify a service worth paying for.
  3. Independent reviewers examine the core security model and verify fixes.
  4. Deployment, support and unit economics are measured in real operating environments.
  5. Any later public network passes its separate governance, custody and operator readiness decisions.
