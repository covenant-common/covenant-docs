Help build the agreement layer for AI work.

Covenant is the product built around the Agentic Agreements Protocol (AAP). It brings the terms, approvals, delivery records and remaining duties together when work crosses agents, teams and applications.

Work crosses systems. The agreement gets scattered.

A brief lives in a conversation, approval arrives by email and delivery appears in another tool. A changed price, a missing reply or an agent restart can leave people unsure what was approved and what still needs doing.

The opportunity is to keep an agreement useful throughout the work, including its changes and problems. The record must remain understandable when it moves between applications.

Keep the agreement useful throughout the work.

One offer and acceptance can be enough for a small job. A longer relationship may need spending limits, recurring orders, milestones, delivery evidence and an agreed review process. Covenant is being developed to connect those parts without requiring a blockchain or project token for basic agreements.

For example, an agent service could commission a report within a customer's budget, ask for approval when the price changes, record a partial delivery and check an uncertain action before retrying it. A pilot must establish how this works with the customer's actual systems. See the capabilities and their current limits.

The likely first buyer builds services for other people.

Our initial commercial focus is developers and platforms building agent services: businesses that need agreement infrastructure and help integrating, deploying and supporting it. Research and analysis are useful first workflows because a brief, price, delivery and reviewer can be made concrete.

A small shop buying one report is an example end user. The proposed annual licence is for the developer or platform running the infrastructure across its service, rather than a charge for that individual deal.

Licensing and support are the proposed business.

The proposed beta licence is USD 7,200 a year for one deployment with five executors included. A deployment is an agreed installation; executors carry out approved actions. Integration, operation and other services need an agreed scope. See pricing, examples and commercial terms.

Commercial use of covered core code needs a paid written licence, including business evaluation and internal use. Earlier licence grants and third-party licences remain in force. The public documentation and browser demo keep their separate MIT licences.

What could make it valuable?

A reusable connection could support more than one service. A continuing agreement could support many orders. Compatible applications could make the same record useful in more places. Those are hypotheses to test, not evidence of adoption or a proven network effect.

Customers will need a reason to choose this over their existing approval, contract and workflow tools: less time reconciling records, fewer unclear handoffs or safer recovery from an interruption. Setup and support must cost less than the value created.

What to examine before investing

  • A working walkthrough. Follow an offer, a separately approved change, a delivery and a problem. See which parts run in the browser demo and which need the full implementation or an integration.
  • Existing test evidence. Review the checks already run, the software version and environment they cover, unresolved failures and what has not been tested. Local tests do not establish independent security or production performance.
  • A proposed pilot plan. Name the workflow owner, participants, setup, costs and success measures before a trial. Measure completion, recovery, time spent clarifying work and willingness to use it again.
  • The commercial case. Test who will pay, the cost to deploy and support them, and whether useful work repeats.
