Help build the agreement layer for AI work.

AI agents can find suppliers, arrange tasks and use services. People still need to know what was agreed, who approved it and what remains owed. AAP / Covenant brings those answers together.

Speak with the foundersBecome a design partner

Work crosses systems. The agreement gets scattered.

A brief lives in a conversation, approval arrives by email and delivery appears in another tool. When the price changes or a reply goes missing, someone has to piece the job back together.

More agents doing more work makes that problem worth solving.

Keep the deal together.

AAP connects the terms, approvals, delivery records and next steps. People can see what changed and carry the agreement into another compatible application.

Start with one offer and acceptance. Add spending limits, staged work, privacy choices and a dispute process when the job needs them. Basic agreements do not require a blockchain or project token.

Begin with a task a customer already needs.

Our first focus is small digital services such as research and analysis: work with a clear brief, a price and a person who reviews the result. A pilot can test the whole relationship, including a change or a missing reply.

A business built on licensing and service.

Covered core code requires a paid written licence for commercial use, including business evaluation and internal use. Earlier licence grants and third-party licences remain in force. The public documentation and browser demo keep their separate MIT licences.

Potential paid services include integration, deployment, ongoing operation and support. Each engagement needs clear responsibilities and agreed terms. See proposed pricing and licensing information.

Useful work can be reused.

A connection built for one service could support another. A successful agreement could become a starting point for the next job. More participating applications could make the same records useful in more places.

That is the opportunity we want to test. Customers will need a good reason to adopt the product and keep using it.

What comes next

  1. Complete a useful pilot with real participants.
  2. Finish independent security review and the setup needed for that use.
  3. Make the experience easier to repeat.
  4. Establish whether customers will pay for it.

The questions that matter

Does it save enough time or prevent enough confusion to justify adoption? Can we make setup straightforward? Can we provide reliable service at a sustainable cost?

We are at an early stage. Production readiness, repeat use and customer demand still need to be demonstrated.
