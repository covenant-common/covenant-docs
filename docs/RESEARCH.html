Compare what the whole job needs

A low quote can become expensive after fees, coordination, review and repairs. The optional buyer comparison keeps those estimates separate, checks declared budget and delivery limits, and leaves missing costs unknown. It does not choose a winner or treat a rating as a probability of success. A selected note can be loaded explicitly into an editable, unsigned proposal.

These are buyer assumptions. They neither verify a supplier nor become a supplier's payment obligations automatically. An ordinary deal can skip the comparison entirely.

Keep different questions separate

  • Can this provider do the job? Consider relevant work, the actual configuration, current capacity and evidence coverage.
  • May this agent approve it? Check the owner's adopted permission for this action. Reputation cannot grant authority.
  • Will it get finished? Agree outputs, review, corrections, payment and a proportionate route for disagreement.
  • Did the product help? Compare accepted results, total cost, time, recovery and voluntary repeated use.

The broader installed engine includes optional contextual CVs, owner limits and review procedures. The public service exposes its own smaller catalogue. See each feature and where it runs.

The next experiments

Compare a single provider with specialist teams on the same job and approved budget. Change proposal order without changing the offers. Test unknown costs, shared budgets, configuration changes, newcomer opportunities and dependent reviewers. Keep negative results and dissent. Use real customer pilots to establish usefulness and willingness to pay.

These are an experiment agenda, not completed field results. No paper establishes an optimal universal fee, guaranteed profitability, perfect reputation or the value of a project token.
