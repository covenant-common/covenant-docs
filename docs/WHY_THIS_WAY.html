Stage names

The same stages on every page.

  1. Find
  2. Agree PROPOSED
  3. Pay BOUND
  4. Deliver DELIVERED
  5. Resolve SETTLED or DISPUTED then RELEASED

Those stage names are the installed work-order checks. The public host cannot settle, hold or refund. A recorded offer or counter-offer is coming soon. The browser sample uses test funds only.

Why we built it this way

This page says why the limits on Scaling Trust limits are shaped as they are. Each reason cites a post or paper that was read on 9 Nov 2026. The full notes are testing/scaling-trust/sources.md. The features themselves are planned unless the registry says otherwise. Citing a paper does not make the feature live.

Covenant never holds your money. In test mode, the test rail holds it; for real payments, the payment provider or an escrow contract will.

The seller is who you are buying from. Covenant provides the platform. The seller is the merchant of record.

The platform fee is not set yet. The owner will confirm this.

Only structured fields should bind

Free-form agent chat can hide a second deal inside ordinary sentences. Motwani and co-authors showed that models can collude in secret, and that paraphrasing the text is a weak defence (Secret Collusion among AI Agents, 2024). Schroeder de Witt and co-authors put secret collusion and free-form protocols on the multi-agent security agenda, and they treat MCP as a channel that needs authentication and tamper-evident routing (Open Challenges in Multi-Agent Security, 2025). The Scaling Trust thesis notes steganographic communication as a concern (Scaling Trust programme thesis v2.0, Alex Obadia, 10 Feb 2026; the short announcement is on the blog).

That is why a money-relevant message should be a typed object, and why leftover prose is hashed and ignored by settlement. The typed tool is planned. It is not a public tool today.

Covenant will not keep both private prices

Alex Obadia’s essay Without Intermediaries (26 Aug 2026) warns that a trusted inbox for negotiation becomes a panoptic platform. The same essay asks for oversight by proofs and opened logs, not a permanent feed. Schlegel and Sun describe credible forgetting so a bargaining limit can be used once and then dropped (Conditional Recall, 2025). Séb Krier’s essay treats personal agents as a way to bargain without a new middleman (Coasean Bargaining at Scale, 26 Sep 2025).

A private session that returns only “match” or “no match”, then deletes the sealed bytes in software, is the planned first step. It is not a hardware wipe. Whether a later hardware-backed step is funded is not set yet. The owner will confirm this.

A sandbox needs a published wall

Tomašev, Franklin, Jacobs, Krier and Osindero describe intentional sandbox economies that are either permeable or sealed, with insulation and circuit breakers (Distributional AGI Safety, Dec 2025). A companion paper warns about flash-crash contagion when a practice market leaks into a live one (Virtual Agent Economies, Sep 2025). The Arena update describes customs at the gate and auditable shared rails (Update on the Scaling Trust Arena, 16 Sep 2026).

Covenant’s registry already says the sandbox is test mode and cannot pay real funds. A named permeability profile, with egress, ingress and customs quotas, is planned. It is not a field you can read yet.

Breakers and bonds

The same sandbox papers, and Nicola Greco’s Physical Evals (23 May 2026), treat anomaly cut-outs and skin-in-the-game bonds as safety equipment: stop the loop, and make a challenge cost something. Hammond, Chan, Clifton and co-authors include destabilising dynamics and commitment devices in their risk taxonomy (Multi-Agent Risks from Advanced AI, Feb 2025).

Circuit breakers are planned. A tripped breaker would tell an agent to wait. Dispute bonds are planned. The bond amount is not set yet. The owner will confirm this. Neither one is a tool you can call.

Many issuers, not one score

Chan and co-authors describe agent infrastructure for attribution, certification and commitments that sits outside any one agent (Infrastructure for AI Agents, Jan 2025). Virtual Agent Economies discusses verifiable credentials and decentralised identifiers. Obadia’s intermediary essay argues that power should stay contestable, which a single reputation score would undermine. The portfolio post lists proving facts about counterparties as a programme track (Building the Scaling Trust portfolio, 7 Oct 2026).

Attestations are planned as separate signed claims. Covenant will not publish one score that replaces them. Which issuers count is not set yet.

Incidents and a bundle, not a firehose

Chan and co-authors also list incident reporting and rollbacks as the response layer. Obadia asks for due-process audit trails rather than a live feed of every agent. An incident report would be tied to one deal. An audit bundle would be that deal’s receipts and hashes, readable by the parties, and not a public stream.

Both are planned. The browser sample’s record is not that bundle.

People audit the money protocols

Nicola Greco’s Generative Cryptography (6 Jul 2026) imagines agents choosing or inventing protocols mid-conversation. For money, Covenant keeps a list of human-audited templates and refuses to let an experimental template fund a deal. That catalogue is planned. Agents do not get to invent a payment rail on the public server.
