Choose the parts your application needs.

  • Agreements and delegated work: several parties, approval limits, milestones, guarantees, evidence and dispute procedures.
  • Markets and exchange: listings, bids, auctions and agreed payment, escrow or credit terms.
  • Teams and decisions: groups, roles, proposals and voting under chosen rules.
  • Discovery and reuse: find services and reusable methods; rank them for a particular need.
  • Privacy and proof: choose disclosures and connect an agreed evidence or verification method.
  • Your own layers and adapters: add a specialist workflow, connector or reusable concept.

These are early-access developer building blocks. Real custody, external actions and each integration need their own setup and testing. There is no live public marketplace to join.

Give your users one clear record.

For a research job, that means the brief, price, deadline, approvals and delivered report. If the brief changes or the report is late, both sides can return to the same record.

  • Show the terms before someone approves them.
  • Make changes easy to compare and approve.
  • Track work that is finished, awaiting review or still owed.
  • Keep the information needed to resolve a disagreement.

Keep the tools your customers use.

Connect through MCP, the SDK or selected A2A workflows, and use files or an adapter for the services your customers need. Basic agreements need neither a blockchain nor a project token. Each real payment, identity, data or device connection must be installed, authorised and tested for its task.

Ask which adapters and versions are available in the approved build. Physical devices and payment providers have separate operating requirements. See integration options and portable and offline records.

Start simple. Add what the job needs.

Begin with an offer and acceptance. A more involved job can add approval limits, stages, evidence, privacy choices and a process for disagreements. Choose the parts that fit your application.

Identity checks and sharing are optional. Agree what information each participant needs instead of collecting everything by default.

A useful first project

  1. Let two people agree on one small piece of work.
  2. Save the agreement and open it in another application.
  3. Change the price or scope and ask for fresh approval.
  4. Show what has been delivered and what still needs attention.

Try the examples first, then follow the developer setup path. For a larger first project, choose a marketplace, staged supplier relationship or custom connector and agree one testable result for the pilot.
