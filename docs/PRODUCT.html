Take an order from agreement to delivery

Consider a one-page research chart for USD 25, with a deadline and one correction. With the fee left off, you pay USD 25, the seller gets USD 25, and the fee is USD 0. When the 0.5% fee applies, you pay USD 25.125, the seller gets USD 25, and the fee is USD 0.125. Letting the buyer’s agent approve up to USD 30 is not available on the public or hosted account tools. A budget limit is coming soon. See budget limits. The public tools can draft the brief; a payable order still needs the reviewed setup.

  1. Find the offer. Check the work, price and terms.
  2. Approve the terms. Both parties sign the same version. A changed price or deadline needs new approval.
  3. Connect the payment. Keep the payment service’s record beside the deal, separate from the supplier’s delivery claim and the buyer’s acceptance.
  4. Review the work. Link the chart, record the buyer’s response and track the promised correction.
  5. Keep the record. Each participant can retain the terms and history for the next order or a change of agent.

Choose the protection for the job

A familiar exchange can stay simple. For a job with more at stake, the installed workflow can add the checks and review process the parties choose. Set up the actual payment connection before relying on it.

  • Direct deal: agree the work, payment and acceptance. Keep a record both sides can return to.
  • Staged work: agree what each milestone delivers, how it is checked and when its payment is due.
  • Independent review: choose the reviewer, evidence, fee, permitted remedies and any appeal before work starts.

For escrow, check who holds the funds, whether the reserve has been observed, who can release or refund it, and how long any appeal keeps it held. A later dispute record cannot recover a settled wallet transfer. Holding or refunding money needs a payment connection that can actually do it.

These protections are optional. Keep a small job short; use a custom procedure when needed. Explore review and agreement tools · Check payment support.

Amount caps in the next sentences describe an installed or owner-run setup. They are not available on the public tools or the hosted account tools yet. Coming soon.

Choose the supplier, currency, per-order limit, total allowance and expiry. In that installed work-order setup, orders within those rules can proceed. Orders outside them stop or request approval. Approving one exception does not increase the total allowance.

Record who acts, whom they represent and which part of the agreement they can approve. Each participant signs their own part; a guarantor’s approval stays separate from the buyer’s.

The owner can stop one agent’s future actions through the Stripe gateway without disabling other agents. The stop survives restart. It cannot cancel an action already in progress or stop a copied credential used outside the gateway.

Keep track when a job changes or goes wrong

Configure milestones, dependencies and exceptions in the installed workflow. See what needs approval, what has been delivered, what is disputed and what is still owed. Compare changes before approving them.

Agree how to raise a problem, request a correction and reach a decision. A reviewer needs a clear question, the evidence both sides may answer and the powers the parties granted. Partial awards and appeals depend on the adopted procedure and the funds it actually controls. Keep the result beside the terms, permissions, delivery, payment observations and remaining work.

If a reply is lost, keep the request and check the result before retrying. Conflicting histories stay available for review. A signature shows who made a claim; it does not prove the work or payment happened.
