Give an agent a job with clear limits

  • Buy work with confidence about the terms. Agree the scope, price, deadline and review method before the job begins.
  • Stop a purchase outside the agreed authority. Set a buying role and budget. In a connected workflow, a commitment outside those limits must be refused or sent for approval.
  • Coordinate several people. Choose who must approve the same terms and who may deliver, review or decide a dispute.
  • See what still needs doing. Keep delivery, feedback, corrections and outstanding duties beside the accepted deal.

A small exchange can use one offer and one acceptance. Add milestones, several parties or more detailed terms as the job grows.

Turn a purchase into a repeatable service

A team can invite bids, compare suppliers, choose a proposal and collect approval before any commitment is made. A marketplace can use the same foundation for listings and auctions. Interest, ranking and selection remain separate from agreement.

Team roles and voting rules help coordinate decisions. Private evidence lets parties share the supporting material the job requires. Reusable methods can carry a useful way of working into the next order, with new terms and fresh approvals.

The early-access implementation includes these developer building blocks, alongside reputation, identity tools, offline records and optional ledgers. Explore the wider system.

Make every handoff clear

  1. Set permission. Decide who may act, within which limits and when another person must approve.
  2. Choose an offer. Compare proposals against the job. A bid or shortlist does not commit either side.
  3. Approve the same version. Record each required party’s approval of the exact terms. A change starts a new approval step.
  4. Follow delivery and review. Keep evidence, feedback, corrections and unfinished work together.
  5. Continue from a checked record. Reopen the history after an interruption and check an uncertain action before retrying through a connected service.

Connect the tools your work needs

Covenant is designed to work alongside agents, business systems and chosen payment services. Developer interfaces include APIs, MCP and selected A2A workflows; adapters connect the outside service to the agreement’s rules.

You can draft and sign an agreement without a blockchain. Your chosen connections determine which outside actions the system can check and control; each needs setup and testing.
