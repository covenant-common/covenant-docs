Bring agreements into the services your customers use.

Covenant is the product built around the Agentic Agreements Protocol (AAP). We’re inviting agent service developers, platforms, marketplaces and service providers to test one useful connection.

Email a partnership outlineExplore possible workflowsPlan a small pilot

Start with one shared job.

Choose work that becomes difficult when it crosses teams or companies: buying a service, managing recurring orders, licensing data or resolving an interrupted delivery. Agree what your service will do, what Covenant will record and what still needs a person.

Agent platforms and business software

Test a service in which an agent can commission work within a budget, ask for approval when terms change and keep the accepted version alongside delivery records. The likely infrastructure buyer is the developer or platform operating that service for its customers.

Marketplaces and purchasing teams

Follow the buyer’s request through offers, changes, milestones and review. Test whether both sides can keep a useful record when part of the work moves outside your interface.

Payment providers

Explore how to connect a payment record to its job and distinguish a request, an uncertain result and an observed completed payment. After a lost reply, the workflow should establish what happened before retrying.

Real payments require a separately agreed provider connection, permissions and responsibilities. The browser demo does not move money.

Identity and evidence services

Test how participants share an identity check, delivery receipt or inspection result for a particular task. A signed record alone does not establish the signer’s real identity or prove that the work was performed.

Researchers and institutions

Study whether people can understand approvals, compare records and recover from mistakes across applications. Agree the question, data, method and publication permissions before research begins.

Keep the first project small.

Agree the participants, integration, information to share, success measures and who helps when the result is unclear. Begin with sample information and examine the ordinary and awkward cases before considering a wider trial.

Agree access, costs and responsibilities.

Possible projects include pilots, integrations, sponsored research and support. Commercial use of covered core code needs a paid written licence; earlier grants and third-party licences retain their rights. Deployment, test and backup instances, setup work and service commitments must be agreed for the project. See proposed pricing and licensing information.
